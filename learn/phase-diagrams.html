DRAFT AREA II

Phase Diagrams

Pressure–temperature diagrams, triple/critical points, boundaries, and water’s unusual melting line.

Why it matters: The supplied past tests repeatedly use labeled phase diagrams and ask for phases, transitions, normal melting/boiling points, and special points.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

How to read a P–T diagram

  • Each region represents the stable phase. Each boundary line represents coexistence of two phases in equilibrium.
  • At 1 atm, the intersections with boundaries give normal melting and normal boiling temperatures.

Triple and critical points

  • The triple point is where solid, liquid, and gas coexist.
  • The critical point is the end of the liquid–gas coexistence line. Above it, liquid and gas are no longer distinct phases.

Water’s negative solid–liquid slope

  • Ordinary ice is less dense than liquid water. Increasing pressure favors the denser liquid, so water’s melting temperature decreases slightly with increasing pressure.

Formula / memory box

Mostly interpretation rather than a single formula. Read axes, regions, and boundary crossings carefully.

Worked example

Prompt

A constant-pressure path crosses from liquid into gas.

Solution

That boundary crossing is vaporization if temperature increases, condensation if the path is reversed.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

What phases coexist at the triple point?
Answer: Solid, liquid, and gas.
What happens to the liquid/gas distinction above the critical point?
Answer: It disappears; the substance is a supercritical fluid.
At fixed pressure, crossing liquid→gas is called what?
Answer: Vaporization.
Why does water’s solid–liquid line slope negatively?
Answer: Liquid water is denser than ice, so pressure favors the liquid phase.
What is a normal boiling point?
Answer: The temperature where the liquid–gas boundary occurs at 1 atm.
What does a boundary line itself represent?
Answer: Two phases coexisting in equilibrium.
