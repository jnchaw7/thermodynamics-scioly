DRAFT AREA III

Conduction, Convection & Radiation

Identify the three heat-transfer modes and reason about real systems.

Why it matters: These are common quick MCQs and short-answer questions, and they also matter directly for the device portion of the event.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Conduction

  • Conduction transfers energy through microscopic interactions within matter, without bulk transport of the material.
  • Metals are often good conductors because mobile electrons transport energy efficiently.

Convection

  • Convection transfers energy by bulk motion of a fluid. It may be natural (buoyancy-driven) or forced (fan/pump-driven).

Radiation

  • Thermal radiation is electromagnetic emission. It does not require matter and can cross a vacuum.
  • Real situations often involve all three mechanisms at once; identify the dominant one from the wording.

Formula / memory box

This guide is mostly conceptual. Quantitative conduction and radiation are treated in separate guides.

Worked example

Prompt

Sunlight warms Earth through empty space.

Solution

Radiation, because electromagnetic waves can travel through vacuum.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

A metal spoon carries energy from its hot end to its cool end.
Answer: Conduction.
Warm air rises above a heater.
Answer: Natural convection.
A fan pushes warm air across a room.
Answer: Forced convection.
Which mode crosses a vacuum?
Answer: Radiation.
Does convection require a fluid?
Answer: Yes.
Why can a metal surface feel colder than wood at the same temperature?
Answer: It usually transfers heat from your skin faster because of higher thermal conductivity/effusivity.
