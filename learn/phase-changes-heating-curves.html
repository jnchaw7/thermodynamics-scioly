DRAFT AREA II

Phase Changes & Heating Curves

Sensible heat, latent heat, heating curves, and multistep energy problems.

Why it matters: Heating-curve diagrams and multistage ice/water/steam calculations appear often in Science Olympiad-style tests.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Six phase changes

  • Solid→liquid: melting/fusion. Liquid→solid: freezing. Liquid→gas: vaporization. Gas→liquid: condensation. Solid→gas: sublimation. Gas→solid: deposition.

Slopes vs plateaus

  • On a heating curve, sloped regions correspond to one phase warming: use Q=mcΔT.
  • Plateaus correspond to a phase change at nearly constant temperature: use Q=mL.
  • The flat segment does not mean no energy is entering; the energy is changing phase rather than raising temperature.

Multistep problems

  • Break the path at every phase boundary. Compute each energy separately and add them.
  • Example path ice below 0 °C → water above 0 °C: warm ice + melt ice + warm liquid water.

Formula / memory box

Q = mcΔTQ = mLQtotal = Σ Qstage

Worked example

Prompt

10 g ice at 0 °C melts. Use Lf=334 J/g.

Solution

Q=(10)(334)=3340 J=3.34 kJ.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

Name gas→solid.
Answer: Deposition.
Which equation is used on a heating-curve plateau?
Answer: Q=mL.
Which equation is used on a sloped segment?
Answer: Q=mcΔT.
20 g ice melts at 0 °C with Lf=334 J/g. Find Q.
Answer: 6.68 kJ.
Why is temperature nearly constant during melting?
Answer: Added energy goes into the phase change/intermolecular configuration rather than mainly increasing average kinetic energy.
List the stages for ice at −10 °C ending as water at 30 °C.
Answer: Warm ice to 0 °C; melt; warm liquid water to 30 °C.
