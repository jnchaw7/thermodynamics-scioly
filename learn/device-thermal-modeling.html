Device-Relevant Thermal Modeling

How calorimetry, conduction, convection, and radiation connect to the SciOly heating device.

Why it matters: The written test is only part of Thermodynamics. Understanding the same heat-transfer ideas also helps you reason about why a collector/insulation design works.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Energy entering the water

  • The water temperature rises because net energy reaches the water/cup system. Useful paths include absorbed lamp radiation followed by conduction into the cup/water.
  • Unwanted paths include radiation and convection losses to the room and conduction into structural supports.

Why surfaces matter

  • Radiative absorptivity/emissivity, orientation, and view of the lamp matter for radiant heating.
  • Insulation reduces conductive and convective losses; low thermal conductivity and trapped air are common reasons insulation works.

Prediction mindset

  • For a fixed setup, heating rate is not exactly constant because losses usually increase as the device warms.
  • A good empirical prediction model records lamp height, heating time, starting temperature, and final temperature under repeatable conditions.

Formula / memory box

Qwater ≈ mcΔTPcond = kAΔT/LPrad ∝ εAT⁴ (for emission)Energy balance: input − losses = rate of stored thermal energy

Worked example

Prompt

Why can shiny reflective material help in one location but hurt in another?

Solution

It can redirect incoming lamp radiation toward the absorber, but a reflective surface used where absorption is desired may reduce absorbed radiant energy.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

What quantity directly relates water temperature rise to absorbed energy?
Answer: Q=mcΔT.
Does lower insulation k generally increase or decrease conductive loss?
Answer: Decrease.
As a device warms above room temperature, do heat losses usually increase or decrease?
Answer: Increase.
Why is repeatability important for prediction?
Answer: The scoring prediction depends on how consistently the same setup maps conditions to final temperature.
What heat-transfer mode carries lamp energy across air even without contact?
Answer: Radiation.
Why should you distinguish an absorber from a reflector?
Answer: An absorber should take in radiant energy; a reflector should redirect it. The desired surface property depends on its role.
