DRAFT AREA III

Specific Heat, Heat Capacity & Calorimetry

Q=mcΔT, heat capacity, energy conservation, mixing problems, calorimeters.

Why it matters: Calorimetry is one of the highest-yield calculation areas. Several supplied past tests use mixing, unknown-specific-heat, and calorimeter-capacity problems.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Heat capacity vs specific heat

  • Heat capacity C tells how much energy an entire object needs per degree of temperature change. Specific heat c is heat capacity per unit mass.
  • For a uniform sample, C = mc. The units of c might be J/(g·K) or J/(kg·K), so always match mass units.

Calorimetry energy balance

  • In an ideal insulated calorimeter, energy lost by hotter parts equals energy gained by colder parts.
  • Use signed heat terms and write ΣQ = 0. Every object that changes temperature can contribute a term.
  • If the calorimeter itself has heat capacity Ccal, include Qcal = CcalΔT.

Strategy

  • 1. Identify every object that changes temperature. 2. Choose one final temperature. 3. Write one Q term per object. 4. Set the algebraic sum to zero. 5. Check that the final temperature lies physically between appropriate initial temperatures unless phase change occurs.

Formula / memory box

Q = mcΔTC = Q/ΔTC = mcΣQ = 0 (ideal isolated calorimetry)

Worked example

Prompt

50 g water at 80 °C is mixed with 100 g water at 20 °C. Ignore the cup.

Solution

Because both are water, Tf=(50·80+100·20)/150=40 °C.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

A 40 g sample with c=0.50 J/(g·K) warms by 20 K. Find Q.
Answer: 400 J.
What is the heat capacity of a 200 g sample with c=0.90 J/(g·K)?
Answer: 180 J/K.
In an ideal calorimeter, what must ΣQ equal?
Answer: 0.
Hot water loses 500 J in an ideal two-object calorimeter. How much heat does the cold object gain?
Answer: 500 J.
Why must a calorimeter’s own heat capacity sometimes be included?
Answer: The calorimeter can absorb or release energy as its temperature changes.
If two equal masses of the same liquid at 20 °C and 80 °C are mixed ideally, find Tf.
Answer: 50 °C.
