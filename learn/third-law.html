DRAFT AREA VI

Third Law & Absolute Zero

Entropy behavior near 0 K and the unattainability of absolute zero.

Why it matters: The draft pairs the third law with blackbody radiation as a state/national topic for both divisions.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Third-law statement

  • For a perfect crystal, entropy approaches a constant conventionally taken as zero as temperature approaches absolute zero.
  • This gives a reference point for absolute entropy.

Absolute zero

  • 0 K is absolute zero on the thermodynamic scale.
  • A related formulation says absolute zero cannot be reached by a finite sequence of thermodynamic operations.

What not to say

  • Do not say “all motion stops” as a universal microscopic statement. Quantum systems retain zero-point effects; the competition-level third law is about thermodynamic entropy and attainability.

Formula / memory box

T → 0 K ⇒ S(perfect crystal) → constant (conventionally 0)

Worked example

Prompt

What happens to the entropy of an ideal perfect crystal as T→0 K?

Solution

It approaches zero under the conventional reference.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

What temperature is absolute zero in kelvins?
Answer: 0 K.
What happens to perfect-crystal entropy as T→0 K?
Answer: It approaches a constant conventionally taken as zero.
Can absolute zero be reached in a finite number of ordinary thermodynamic processes?
Answer: No.
Is the third law the same as conservation of energy?
Answer: No; conservation of energy is the first law.
Which law provides an absolute entropy reference?
Answer: The third law.
Why is “all motion stops” not a precise statement?
Answer: Quantum systems can retain zero-point motion; the third law is formulated thermodynamically.
