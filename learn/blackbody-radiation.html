DRAFT AREA VI

Blackbody Radiation & Stefan–Boltzmann Law

Emissivity, radiant exitance, total power, T⁴ scaling, and Wien’s law as useful extension.

Why it matters: The state/national draft includes radiant exitance, blackbody radiation, and the Stefan–Boltzmann law. Older tests also use Wien-law calculations.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Blackbody and emissivity

  • An ideal blackbody absorbs all incident radiation and is the maximum possible thermal emitter at a given temperature.
  • Emissivity ε describes how strongly a real surface emits compared with a blackbody. For a gray surface, 0≤ε≤1.

Stefan–Boltzmann law

  • Radiant exitance M is emitted power per unit area: M=εσT⁴.
  • Total emitted power is P=εσAT⁴. Temperature must be in kelvins.
  • Because of the fourth power, modest temperature changes can cause large radiation changes.

Wien’s displacement law

  • Wien’s law gives the wavelength where a blackbody spectrum peaks: λmaxT=b.
  • It is useful background and has appeared in older Science Olympiad-style thermodynamics tests even when not always called out in the draft line-by-line.

Formula / memory box

M = εσT⁴P = εσAT⁴σ = 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ W·m⁻²·K⁻⁴λmaxT ≈ 2.898×10⁻³ m·K

Worked example

Prompt

Blackbody at 300 K: estimate M.

Solution

M=σ(300)⁴≈459 W/m².

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

What is ε for an ideal blackbody?
Answer: 1.
What are the units of radiant exitance?
Answer: W/m².
If T doubles, by what factor does blackbody exitance change?
Answer: 16.
What extra factor turns exitance into total power?
Answer: Surface area A.
Why must T be in K?
Answer: The law depends on absolute thermodynamic temperature.
Using Wien’s law, a hotter blackbody peaks at a shorter or longer wavelength?
Answer: Shorter wavelength.
