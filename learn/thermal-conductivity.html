DRAFT AREA III

Thermal Conductivity & Steady Conduction

Fourier conduction, heat flux, geometry, insulation, and units.

Why it matters: The draft explicitly names thermal conductivity, and the old tests include slab/rod heat-flow calculations.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

What k means

  • Thermal conductivity k is a material property that measures how readily heat is conducted.
  • Large k means a larger heat-transfer rate for the same geometry and temperature difference.

One-dimensional slab model

  • For steady conduction through a uniform slab, P=kAΔT/L.
  • Heat flux is power per area: P/A=kΔT/L.
  • The model assumes a steady temperature profile and essentially one-dimensional conduction.

Scaling

  • Doubling area doubles P. Doubling ΔT doubles P. Doubling thickness halves P. Changing k scales P directly.

Formula / memory box

P = Q/t = kAΔT/LHeat flux = P/A = kΔT/L[k] = W/(m·K)

Worked example

Prompt

k=0.50 W/(m·K), A=0.20 m², ΔT=10 K, L=0.05 m.

Solution

P=(0.50)(0.20)(10)/0.05=20 W.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

What are the SI units of k?
Answer: W/(m·K).
If wall thickness doubles, what happens to steady P?
Answer: It halves.
If area triples, what happens to P?
Answer: It triples.
k=1, A=0.1, ΔT=20, L=0.02. Find P.
Answer: 100 W.
What is heat flux?
Answer: Heat-transfer power per unit area, W/m².
Why is low k useful for insulation?
Answer: It reduces conductive heat-transfer rate for the same geometry and ΔT.
