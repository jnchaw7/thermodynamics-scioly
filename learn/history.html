DRAFT AREA V

History of Thermodynamics

Kelvin, Black, Joule, Carnot, Planck, Clausius, Boltzmann, and Maxwell.

Why it matters: The current draft explicitly names these eight people, and the old tests frequently ask “who did what?” questions.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Early heat concepts

  • Joseph Black is strongly associated with specific heat and latent heat.
  • Sadi Carnot analyzed the performance of heat engines and inspired the ideal reversible cycle bearing his name.

Energy and the second law

  • James Prescott Joule established the quantitative equivalence between mechanical work and heat through careful experiments.
  • Rudolf Clausius gave a major formulation of the second law and introduced the entropy concept/name.
  • Lord Kelvin developed the absolute thermodynamic temperature scale and helped put thermodynamics on a rigorous foundation.

Statistical and quantum developments

  • James Clerk Maxwell made foundational contributions to kinetic theory and molecular speed distributions.
  • Ludwig Boltzmann connected macroscopic thermodynamics to microscopic statistics, especially entropy.
  • Max Planck’s blackbody work led to the quantum hypothesis.

Formula / memory box

Memory target: person ↔ signature contribution. Exact dates are less important than the sequence and idea.

Worked example

Prompt

Question: Who is associated with the mechanical equivalent of heat?

Solution

James Prescott Joule.

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

Who is associated with latent heat and specific heat?
Answer: Joseph Black.
Who developed the reversible ideal heat-engine cycle?
Answer: Sadi Carnot.
Who established the mechanical equivalent of heat?
Answer: James Prescott Joule.
Who is strongly associated with entropy and the second law?
Answer: Rudolf Clausius.
Who developed the absolute thermodynamic temperature scale?
Answer: Lord Kelvin / William Thomson.
Who are the two names in the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution?
Answer: James Clerk Maxwell and Ludwig Boltzmann.
Who is associated with the blackbody quantum hypothesis?
Answer: Max Planck.
