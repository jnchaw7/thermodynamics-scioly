DRAFT AREA II

Ideal Gas Law

PV=nRT, unit discipline, state variables, and competition calculation patterns.

Why it matters: The draft specifically names the ideal gas law, and old tests sometimes ask both direct calculations and conceptual process questions.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Equation of state

  • For an ideal gas, pressure, volume, amount, and absolute temperature are related by PV=nRT.
  • Temperature must be in kelvins. Use a version of R consistent with your units.

Unit strategy

  • With R=8.314 J/(mol·K), use pressure in pascals and volume in cubic meters because 1 J = 1 Pa·m³.
  • If you keep volume in liters, use a compatible R value such as 0.082057 L·atm/(mol·K).

Physical interpretation

  • At fixed n and V, P is proportional to T. At fixed n and T, P is inversely proportional to V.
  • The ideal-gas model works best at low density and away from condensation.

Formula / memory box

PV = nRTP1V1/T1 = P2V2/T2 for fixed n1 L = 1×10⁻³ m³

Worked example

Prompt

1.00 mol gas at 300 K occupies 10.0 L. Find P with R=8.314.

Solution

P=(1)(8.314)(300)/(0.0100)=2.49×10⁵ Pa=249 kPa.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

Why must T be in kelvins?
Answer: The gas-law proportionality uses absolute thermodynamic temperature.
At fixed V and n, if T doubles, what happens to P?
Answer: It doubles.
At fixed T and n, if V doubles, what happens to P?
Answer: It halves.
Convert 5 L to m³.
Answer: 0.005 m³.
What are the SI units of R=8.314?
Answer: J/(mol·K).
1 mol at 300 K in 24.9 L has about what pressure?
Answer: About 100 kPa.
