DRAFT AREA IV

Thermodynamic Processes & the First Law

Isothermal/isobaric/isochoric/adiabatic processes, work, and ΔU=Q−W.

Why it matters: Process identification and first-law calculations are core competition material and feed directly into cycles and engines.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

Four named processes

  • Isothermal: T constant. Isobaric: P constant. Isochoric/isovolumetric: V constant. Adiabatic: Q=0.
  • Names tell you what constraint holds, not necessarily whether other variables change.

First law sign convention

  • This site uses ΔU=Q−W, where Q>0 means heat enters the system and W>0 means the system does work on the surroundings.
  • Always check the convention stated by a problem; chemistry resources sometimes use a different sign for work.

PV work

  • For constant pressure, W=PΔV. Expansion gives positive W by the system; compression gives negative W under this convention.
  • For an isochoric process, ΔV=0, so PV boundary work is zero.

Formula / memory box

ΔU = Q − WW = PΔV (constant P)Adiabatic: Q=0Isochoric PV work: W=0

Worked example

Prompt

A gas absorbs 600 J and does 250 J of work.

Solution

ΔU=600−250=350 J.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

Which process has Q=0?
Answer: Adiabatic.
Which process has V constant?
Answer: Isochoric/isovolumetric.
A gas absorbs 500 J and does 200 J work. Find ΔU.
Answer: 300 J.
At constant pressure 100 kPa, a gas expands by 2 L. Find W.
Answer: 200 J, because 1 kPa·L = 1 J.
For an isochoric process, what is PV boundary work?
Answer: 0.
What sign is W for compression under the site convention?
Answer: Negative, because work is done on the gas rather than by it.
