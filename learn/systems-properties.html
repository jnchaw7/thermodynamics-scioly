DRAFT AREA I

Thermodynamic Systems & Properties

Open, closed, and isolated systems; boundaries; intensive vs extensive properties.

Why it matters: This is the language behind almost every other thermodynamics question. Competition tests often ask definitions directly or hide them inside a scenario.
Practice this rule areaJump to mini quiz

System, surroundings, and boundary

  • A thermodynamic system is the part of the universe you choose to analyze. Everything else is the surroundings. The boundary can be real or imaginary.
  • An open system can exchange mass and energy. A closed system has fixed mass but can exchange energy. An isolated system exchanges neither mass nor energy.
  • A rigid boundary prevents volume change; an adiabatic boundary prevents heat transfer. Those are separate ideas from open/closed/isolated.

Intensive vs extensive properties

  • Intensive properties do not scale with system size: temperature, pressure, density, specific heat, thermal conductivity.
  • Extensive properties scale with amount of matter: mass, volume, total internal energy, total entropy, number of moles.
  • Ratios of extensive properties can become intensive. Density = mass/volume is the classic example.

Equilibrium

  • Thermal equilibrium means no net heat flow between systems in thermal contact.
  • Mechanical, chemical, and thermal equilibrium are different conditions; thermodynamic equilibrium requires all relevant macroscopic conditions to stop changing.

Formula / memory box

No single formula dominates this subsection. Focus on definitions and boundary reasoning.

Worked example

Prompt

A sealed aluminum can is heated on a hot plate. Mass cannot cross the lid, but heat can cross the wall.

Solution

The contents are a closed system, not isolated.

MINI QUIZ

Practice before you leave

Try each one without opening the answer. Then reveal it to self-check.

A turbine has fluid entering and leaving. Open, closed, or isolated?
Answer: Open.
Is density intensive or extensive?
Answer: Intensive.
Is total internal energy intensive or extensive?
Answer: Extensive.
Can a closed system do boundary work?
Answer: Yes. Closed means no mass crosses; energy may cross as work or heat.
What does an adiabatic wall forbid?
Answer: Heat transfer.
If two bodies are in thermal equilibrium, what is true about net heat flow?
Answer: There is no net heat flow between them.
